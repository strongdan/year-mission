# Year Mission gamification strategy

## Goal

Make execution feel more compelling without turning Year Mission into a streak machine or a generic points app.

The product should reward **starting, meaningful action, recovery, balance, and evidence of change**. It should not reward compulsive checking, trivial task farming, or fear of losing progress.

## Core loops

### 1. Daily Charge

A visible daily energy meter converts meaningful completed work into immediate feedback. High-impact, courage, and Weekly Win work carries more weight. Meta-work does not count.

The meter has calm deterministic states:

- Ready
- Ignited
- In motion
- Day won
- Full charge

The meter resets daily, but durable Mission Garden growth never regresses.

### 2. Comeback recognition

A return after quiet days is explicitly recognized as a comeback. This replaces streak-loss psychology with recovery psychology: coming back is an achievement rather than evidence of failure.

### 3. Bonus mission

The weakest unprotected Big Four category becomes the optional bonus mission. This adds a game-like side quest while reinforcing balance rather than encouraging more total work.

### 4. Deterministic unlocks

Progress reveals the next state and how much meaningful work is needed to reach it. Avoid random loot, variable-ratio rewards, countdown pressure, or artificial scarcity.

### 5. Durable growth

Mission Garden remains the long-horizon progression system. Daily game feedback should point toward durable evidence, milestones, courage, and comebacks rather than replacing them.

## Guardrails

- No streak loss penalties.
- No points for meta-work.
- No infinite feeds or repeated check-in loops.
- No random reward boxes.
- No pay-to-progress mechanics.
- No shame language for quiet days.
- A small number of meaningful actions should be enough to make a day feel successful.
